Most people find their perfume by accident. A gift, a counter, a friend who smelled good. Then they wear it for ten years, not because it is theirs, but because looking again feels like work.

It isn't, if you ask the right questions in the right order. A fragrance stylist doesn't start with bottles. She starts with you.

## Start with what you already wear

The perfume you reach for now is the best clue you have. Not its name, its notes.

Every perfume is built from notes: the citrus or spice that opens it, the flowers or woods at its heart, the amber, musk or vanilla that stays on your skin for hours. When you like a perfume, you rarely like all of it. You like two or three of those notes.

Look up the one you wear. If the base is vanilla and tonka, you are drawn to warmth. If the opening is bergamot and the base is vetiver, you are drawn to clean and dry. That is your starting point, and it is far more useful than "I like fresh scents".

## Then say what you never want to smell

This is the question perfume counters skip, and it is the one that saves the most time.

Some people cannot wear heavy white florals. Some find oud and smoke too much for daytime. Some go sharp at the first hint of sweet. Name those notes first. Every perfume that carries them leaves the table, and what remains is already a short list.

## Match the perfume to your week, not to a mood

"What do you want to smell like?" is a hard question. "Where does your week go?" is an easy one.

An office with a scent-sensitive colleague wants something close to the skin: musks, soft woods, clean florals. Evenings and dates can carry amber, rose, leather. A Sunday at home asks for comfort, not projection. Sport wants citrus and nothing that turns on warm skin.

One perfume rarely covers all of that. This is why we talk about a perfume wardrobe rather than a single signature: two or three bottles, each with a job. The signature is the one you reach for most, and it is easier to choose once the others are in place.

## Decide how noticed you want to be

The same perfume can be a whisper or an announcement, depending on its concentration and its notes. An eau de toilette with citrus and tea stays within arm's reach. An extrait with oud and amber fills a room.

Neither is better. But "I want people to lean in" and "I want to be the only one who knows" lead to different bottles, and it is worth saying which one you mean before you smell anything.

## Try it on skin, and wait

Paper strips show the opening. Skin shows the perfume.

Spray one perfume on one wrist, go about your day and smell it again after an hour, then after four. The notes that stay are the ones you will live with. Two perfumes per day is the honest limit: more than that and your nose stops telling the truth.

This is also why we sell samples before bottles. A 2 ml vial is three or four full wears, enough to know.

- Look up the notes of the perfume you wear now. That is your taste, in words.
- Name the notes you never want. Everything that carries them is out.

## Questions we hear

### How long does it take to find a signature perfume?

With the questions above, a week: a few days to narrow the list, then three or four days of wearing samples. Without them, people take years, mostly because they test at random.

### Should a signature perfume be one bottle or several?

Several, used with intent. One perfume for work, one for evenings and one for home is a wardrobe; the one you wear most becomes your signature. A single bottle for every occasion usually means compromising on all of them.
